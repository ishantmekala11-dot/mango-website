Model United Nations

ENTER THE ROOM.

RESEARCH.REPRESENT.NEGOTIATE.LEAD.

Research a country. Understand its interests. Represent its perspective. Negotiate with people who may disagree. Then defend your position.

01 / 06
01

RESEARCH

Understand the country, the bloc, and the issue before you ever speak.

02

REPRESENT

Step into a perspective that may not be your own and argue it faithfully.

03

NEGOTIATE

Find common ground with delegates whose interests conflict with yours.

04

COLLABORATE

Build coalitions and draft language that a divided room can agree on.

05

RESOLVE

Turn debate into a resolution that actually holds up to scrutiny.

06

LEAD

Carry what you've learned about persuasion and diplomacy beyond the floor.

About

A PLACE TO
THINK OUT LOUD.

MANGO exists because young people already have opinions worth hearing — what they often lack is the room, the research skills, and the confidence to make those opinions land. We built that room.

The Name

What MANGO stands for.

MModern+

Preparing students to engage with the issues shaping today's world.

AAdvocates+

Developing the confidence to communicate ideas and stand behind them.

NNew+

Encouraging curiosity, innovation, and perspectives beyond conventional thinking.

GGlobal+

Understanding issues through international perspectives.

OOpinions+

Building informed perspectives and learning how to communicate them effectively.

Mission, Vision, Values

What drives us.

Mission

To give students the research skills, rhetorical confidence, and diplomatic judgment to turn opinions into informed, persuasive advocacy.

Vision

A generation of young people who engage with global issues critically, communicate them clearly, and lead conversations that matter.

Values

Curiosity, rigor, respect for perspectives unlike our own, and the discipline to back opinions with research.

Our Philosophy
DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVES DON'T WEAKEN A CONVERSATION. THEY MAKE IT WORTH HAVING.

Every delegate MANGO trains has to argue a position they may not personally hold. That discipline — understanding an opposing view well enough to represent it — is the foundation of everything we teach.
